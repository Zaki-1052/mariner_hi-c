SATB2 influences the 3D genome architecture of
cortical neurons at multiple hierarchical levels
To gain insights into the global 3D genome folding pattern upon
SATB2 loss, we explored distance-dependent chromatin inter-
action frequencies. Genome-wide average contacts decayed
more strongly with distance in cKO vs. floxed Hi-C contact
matrices at all distances (Figure 4A). Accordingly, we observed
weakened compartmentalization in cKO neurons of both inac-
tive (B) and active (A) compartments (Figures 4B and 4C). At
the level of topologically associating domains (TADs), the intra-
TAD interaction frequency was decreased in the cKO compared
with floxed Hi-C matrix (Figure 4D). Furthermore, interactions
between neighboring TADs were diminished in cKO compared
with floxed matrices (Figure 4E).
We next identified specific genomic regions that undergo A/B
compartment and TAD rearrangements upon SATB2 deletion. At
100-kb resolution, pairwise differential analysis revealed 1,451 sig-
nificant compartment switches and strength changes between
cKO and floxed neurons, affecting 5.6% of the genome (Figure 5A;
Table S2). The changes in compartment score (DEV1) were posi-
tively correlated (Spearman r2 = 0.34, p < 0.001) with chromatin
accessibility changes (mean log2FC of all OCRs within a differen-
tial compartment). Likewise, the genes within differential compart-
ments (631 genes, Table S3) showed a positive correlation be-
tween compartment score shifts (DEV1) and expression changes
(Figure 5B) and were enriched in postsynaptic, dendritic, and
memory-related GO terms (Figure 5C). Genes linked to synaptic
plasticity, such as Bdnf, Grm7, Fmr1, and Kcnb2, as well as
SCZ-risk-prioritized genes,42 including Csmd1, Lrrc4b, Ptprd,
and Epn2 (the latter also associated with intelligence43), were
among those affected by compartment changes (Figure 5D).
TAD calling44 identified a similar number and average width of
TADs and subTADs in floxed vs. cKO Hi-C matrices (Figures S5A
and S5B). Of all TAD boundaries, 12.7% were identified as differ-
ential (Figure 5E; Table S2), displaying various types of changes
(complex, split, merge, shift, strength, Figure S5C).45 In total,
10.1% of the differential TAD boundaries overlapped with SATB2
peaks. In cKO neurons, the average insulation score decreased
across 692 TAD boundaries (lost TAD boundaries), increased at
511 TAD boundaries (gained TAD boundaries), but did not change
at invariant boundaries (Figure 5F), corroborating the results of the
differential TAD analysis. Genes nearby lost TAD boundaries (504
genes, Table S3) were expressed at lower levels in cKO neurons
(Figure 5G) and were strongly enriched for GO terms related to
glutamate receptor signaling and neuron projection development
(Figure 5H). In contrast, genes nearby gained TAD boundaries (310
genes, Table S3) were expressed at higher levels in cKO neurons
but lacked significant GO term enrichment. Lost but not gained
TAD boundaries were enriched for genomic annotations known
to be associated with TAD boundaries, e.g., promoters and
CTCF binding sites46–48 (Figure 5I).
In summary, our data establish SATB2 as a cell-type-specific
modifier of 3D chromatin structure in pyramidal neurons, acting
not only on chromatin loops but also on multiple larger scale hi-
erarchical levels.
