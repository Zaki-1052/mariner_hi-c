Great instinct — building the story from biological first principles before thinking about presentation structure will make everything downstream stronger. Let me walk through what I see when I strip away all the analytical sophistication and ask: what did you actually observe, and what does it mean?
The Ground Floor: What Was Measured, What Changed
At the most reduced level, you measured one thing: how often pairs of DNA regions physically touch each other in cells with vs without BAP1, at two developmental ages. Everything else is interpretation layered on top of that measurement.
The most basic result is: the pattern of physical contacts changes when BAP1 is gone. That alone is a finding — it wasn’t guaranteed. BAP1 is a histone-modifying enzyme, not a structural protein. It doesn’t directly hold loops together the way cohesin or CTCF do. So the fact that removing it rewires physical genome contacts tells you that the histone modification it controls (H2AK119ub) is somehow load-bearing for 3D structure.
One Layer Deeper: The Changes Aren’t Random
If BAP1 loss just generally destabilized chromatin, you’d expect a noisy, directionless mess — contacts lost everywhere, maybe some gained randomly. That’s not what you see. The changes have a clear geometric signature: contacts that span long distances are preferentially lost, while shorter-distance contacts are preferentially gained. The median lost loop is ~2× farther than the median gained loop. Loops over 1 Mb are 3.3× enriched for loss.
This is the core observation that everything else hangs on. It’s not “BAP1 loss disrupts chromatin” — it’s “BAP1 loss shortens the reach of chromatin contacts.” That’s a much more specific claim, and it immediately raises the question: why would losing a histone deubiquitinase preferentially break long-range contacts?
The Mechanistic Layer: Why Distance Matters
This is where the H2AK119ub data becomes important. BAP1 removes this mark; without BAP1, it accumulates. The key observation is that where H2AK119ub accumulates predicts where contacts are lost — and it does so powerfully (OR = 10.7 in the logistic regression). But the how differs by chromatin context, and I think this is the most biologically interesting part of your data:
At active regulatory elements (enhancers, promoters with H3K27ac), K119ub accumulation is essentially a foreign signal. These sites were being kept “clean” by BAP1 — the enzyme was actively removing ubiquitin that PRC1 was depositing. When BAP1 is gone, the mark builds up and directly weakens the enhancer-promoter contacts at those sites. This works at any distance — it’s a local, site-specific effect.
At Polycomb domains (H3K27me3-marked regions), the story is different. These sites already had H2AK119ub — it was part of their normal identity. But it was being dynamically cycled (deposited by PRC1, removed by BAP1, re-deposited…). When you freeze that cycle by removing BAP1, the long-range Polycomb-Polycomb contacts that these domains maintain begin to collapse. Short-range compaction at these sites actually increases (the positive rho = +0.177 at Polycomb anchors). So it’s not that K119ub is toxic to all contacts — it’s that the turnover of K119ub is what maintained the long-range architecture, and without turnover, contacts redistribute locally.
That’s the reconciliation from your theory.md, and I think it’s the deepest biological insight in the project: it’s not the presence or absence of H2AK119ub that maintains genome architecture — it’s the dynamic cycling of it.
The Temporal Layer: It Gets Worse
The 165 → 2,910 loop amplification from P12 to P60 tells you this isn’t a one-time hit. It’s a progressive cascade. Early on, the genome is mostly holding together — only a whisper of dysregulation. By adulthood, the system has failed at scale. And the direction reverses: early, the dominant signal is loss (existing contacts failing); late, it’s gain (ectopic contacts forming as architecture collapses).
From first principles, this makes sense as a threshold/cascade phenomenon: initial K119ub accumulation weakens some contacts → those structural changes redistribute chromatin → which exposes new regions to aberrant Polycomb marking → which destabilizes more contacts → and so on. The early timepoint is the perturbation; the late timepoint is the consequence of that perturbation propagating through a coupled system.
The Functional Layer: It Matters for Genes
The ABC integration closes the loop (no pun intended): altered contacts → altered enhancer-gene linkages → altered gene expression, with 88% three-way concordance. This says the structural changes aren’t epiphenomenal — they’re driving transcriptional outcomes. The affected genes (synaptic, developmental) are consistent with the neurodegeneration phenotype in BAP1-KO mice.

So What’s the “Most Basic Level” Story?
If I had to state it in one chain:
Removing a histone-modifying enzyme → a specific histone mark accumulates → long-range genome contacts preferentially collapse while short-range contacts form → this rewires which enhancers talk to which genes → developmental and neuronal genes are dysregulated → and it gets progressively worse over the animal’s lifetime.
The surprising part isn’t any single link in that chain — it’s that a histone modification, traditionally thought of as a gene-level regulatory mark, turns out to be structurally load-bearing for genome-scale 3D architecture. And specifically that it’s the turnover of the mark, not just its presence, that matters.
