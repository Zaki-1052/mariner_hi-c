⏺ I've completed the literature review. Here's a summary of what I found:

  Summary: Expected vs. Novel Findings

  EXPECTED (Consistent with Literature)

  1. High sensitivity of cerebellum to methylation changes - Brain has ~40% of
  modified cytosines as 5hmC (Mellén et al. 2012, Cell)
  2. Gene body methylation as primary target - This is where 5hmC is enriched in
   neurons (Ma et al. 2018)
  3. BAP1 loss affects chromatin state - Known to cause H2AK119ub accumulation
  and chromatin compaction (Conway et al. 2021, Molecular Cell)
  4. Neuronal genes are vulnerable - High 5hmC in neurons makes them sensitive;
  Purkinje cells are particularly affected in other epigenetic disorders (Jiang
  et al. 2015)

  NOVEL (Your Contribution)

  1. The coordinated mC↑/hmC↓ pattern - 92.3% concordance at the same loci is
  remarkable and not previously reported for BAP1
  2. The BAP1 → H2AK119ub → TET access model - This mechanistic connection is
  NEW:
  BAP1 loss → H2AK119ub accumulates → Chromatin compacts →
  TET enzymes blocked → 5mC can't be oxidized →
  mC↑ / hmC↓ at same sites
  3. Syt1 (Synaptotagmin-1) as top hit - First link between BAP1 and this
  critical synaptic gene (+18.4% mC, -15.8% hmC)
  4. Active Promoter specificity for methylation effects - 62.7% of DMRs at
  active promoters; 97.1% of hypermethylated DMRs are there

  The Big Picture

  Your data suggests BAP1 is a guardian of DNA methylation homeostasis at
  actively transcribed genes. The proposed mechanism: BAP1 loss creates a
  chromatin environment where TET enzymes cannot access their substrates,
  leading to this striking coordinated signature.

  The full document is at: biomodal/downstream/literature_review.md
