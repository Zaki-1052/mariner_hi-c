## One-line takeaway

BAP1-KO cerebellum has a coordinated, reciprocal **gene-body 5mC-gain / 5hmC-loss** signature with a **flat total modified-cytosine pool** — the molecular fingerprint of a blocked TET-mediated active-demethylation pathway, reproducible across all four replicate pairs and both sexes.

---

## Biological story

This is the "what happened" figure. It establishes the core methylation phenotype before any mechanism is invoked. In the project's causal cascade — **BAP1 loss → H2AK119ub accumulation → 5mC↑ / 5hmC↓ at gene bodies → (downstream: TET-block, MeCP2 redistribution, neuronal dysregulation)** — Figure 1 documents the third link as a measured, genome-scale fact and shows that its shape is diagnostic of the mechanism that follows.

The single most important observation is the *reciprocity*: at the same gene bodies, 5mC rises while 5hmC falls, and the two near-equal-and-opposite shifts leave the total modified-cytosine pool essentially unchanged (Panel A). Because 5hmC is the obligate first oxidation product of 5mC by TET enzymes en route to demethylation, a simultaneous 5mC gain plus 5hmC loss across thousands of gene bodies is best explained not by indiscriminate de-novo hypermethylation but by TET being unable to process 5mC — an active-demethylation block. The phenotype is overwhelmingly a *gene-body* phenomenon (51.4% of gene bodies significant vs 8.3% of promoters, 5.0% of CpG islands, 1.4% of TSS regions; Panel B), it is directionally asymmetric (5mC 70% hyper, 5hmC 83% down; Panels C/E), and at the per-gene level 78.7% of co-significant genes follow the diagnostic mC↑/hmC↓ quadrant (Panel D). The effect is small per gene (medians ≈ +2% / −2%) but exceptionally consistent, which is why the bulk shift carries enormous Cohen's d values despite tiny absolute deltas (Panel A). The phenotype separates conditions cleanly on PC1 while sex emerges only as a secondary axis on PC2 (Panel F), and the entire signature is visible at single-locus resolution at the archetypal synaptic gene **Syt1** (+17.3% 5mC / −15.0% 5hmC, with concurrent H2AK119ub; Panel G). The most-affected genes are neuronal/synaptic, foreshadowing the neuronal specificity developed in later figures.

## Caveats & framing

- **"Coordinated" = 78.7%, not 92.3%.** The canonical, table-confirmed figure for coordinated mC↑/hmC↓ among co-significant genes is **78.7% (6,589 / 8,371)**, recomputed directly from the run-5 BEDs and `coordinated_changes.tsv`. The "92.3%" string still present in several `CLAUDE.md` files is UNVERIFIED against the run-5 tables (likely a different denominator or older run) — do not cite it. Use 78.7%.
- **Do not cite `FIGURES.md` / `TODO.md` numbers for sections 01–09.** Those carry stale older-run values (e.g. 8,836 / 9,930 significant; 6,750 co-significant; 84.6% coordinated; +2.27% / −2.08% means). The canonical run-5 numbers are the ones in this doc and in `docs/results/section_01_09_core_dmr.md` / `summary_statistics.txt`.
- **Panel A relies on the paired t-test, not Wilcoxon.** With n=4 pairs the paired Wilcoxon floors at p=0.125 and is underpowered; the operative significance test for the global shift is the paired t-test (5mC p=0.041, 5hmC p=0.018). The Cohen's d values (the headline) come from the same paired analysis.
- **Panel E medians are computed live (≈ +2.03% / −1.83%), not the plan's literals.** The approved plan table listed stale +3.07% / −2.14% literals; the script instead computes the medians from the actual deduped significant set at runtime, so the annotation is always self-consistent with the plotted density. This is an intentional, verified deviation — the plotted label is the trustworthy number.
- **Panel B mixes one deduped count with five per-feature counts.** "Gene bodies" is deduplicated per-gene (one row/gene) while the other five region classes are raw per-feature counts (multiple features per gene), inherited from `region_dmrs`. This is a methodological inconsistency to note, but it does not change the qualitative "gene bodies dominate" message.
- **H2AK119ub sign reconciliation (for the broader paper, not contradicted within Fig 1).** In later figures K119ub is a *positive* predictor of hypermethylation as a **differential DiffBind fold** (genes that *gain* K119ub hypermethylate) but a *negative* predictor as **baseline absolute signal** in the TET model (genes with high *constitutive* K119ub are already compacted and inaccessible). Figure 1 only displays the K119ub track at Syt1 (a gene that gains both K119ub and methylation), so the two framings are not in tension here — but the distinction must be kept clear when this figure is read alongside Figures 2–3.
- **Neuronal specificity is "preferentially," not "exclusively."** The most-affected genes (Syt1, Cntnap2, etc.) are neuronal/synaptic, but the methylation shift is genome-wide across active chromatin; neuronal genes are disproportionately affected because they are constitutively K119ub-rich, not because the effect is restricted to them. Use "preferentially / disproportionately."
- **Methylation assay vs MeCP2 assay.** Figure 1 is entirely DUET evoC methylation. The H2AK119ub track is ChIP/CUT&RUN signal labelled "H2AK119ub" (the figure contains no MeCP2 panel). MeCP2, when it appears in later figures, is **CUT&RUN** — label it "signal" or "mark," never "ChIP."
- **Panel G / Section 46 has no quantification table** — it is a rendered-tracks view; the Syt1 +17.3% / −15.0% numbers come from `mc_dmr` / `hmc_dmr` / `coordinated_changes.tsv` (verified), not from a Gviz-exported TSV.
