## One-line takeaway

In BAP1-KO cerebellum, the change in MeCP2 binding is governed by **chromatin state — above all the Polycomb mark H2AK119ub — and not by CG DNA methylation**: CG methylation explains <2% of MeCP2 binding while chromatin marks explain ~25%, and at the loci where MeCP2 increases without any methylation change it is K119ub that is gained (OR = 3.15).

---

## Biological story

Figures 1–3 establish the methylation phenotype and its cause: BAP1 loss raises H2AK119ub, which (via a TET-demethylation block at active chromatin) produces the coordinated 5mC↑/5hmC↓ shift. The natural next question — the one Jai's hypothesis frames — is whether MeCP2, the canonical methyl-CpG reader, simply *follows* that methylation change. MeCP2 is a CUT&RUN target here, and BAP1-KO redistributes it. Figure 5 is the verdict: MeCP2 does **not** track CG methylation. It tracks chromatin state, and the single dominant feature of that state is H2AK119ub. The decisive quantitative result is a nested regression at all ~202,574 MeCP2 peaks — CG 5mC/5hmC alone explain only R² = 0.017 of MeCP2 binding, whereas the seven-mark chromatin model explains R² = 0.246 (~15× more), with K119ub the top standardized predictor (β = +0.199). Variance partitioning makes the dissociation visible: chromatin contributes 24.3% of unique explained variance versus 1.5% for CG methylation.

The "show-the-data" core of the figure is the cohort of **359 genes where MeCP2 binding rises with no significant methylation change at all** — mechanistically inconvenient for a pure methyl-reader model. At these loci H2AK119ub is gained in 72.8% of genes versus 45.9% of the genome (Fisher OR = 3.15, p = 1.8e-24): MeCP2 is following the Polycomb/BAP1 axis, decoupled from DNA methylation. This is reinforced by the mirror-image epigenetic profiles that define MeCP2 status — MeCP2-Up loci show the full cascade (K119ub↑, 5mC↑, 5hmC↓, K27ac↓, K27me3↑), while MeCP2-Down loci mirror every mark *except* K119ub, which stays flat (mean −0.04, not significant). That flat-K119ub-at-Down signature is the cleanest single discriminator between the two mechanisms. Finally, a per-gene variance partition of MeCP2 fold adjudicates the gradient question directly: the per-gene 5mC/5hmC change ratio — the magnitude of the TET-blockade skew — explains essentially **0.0%** of MeCP2 fold, whereas K119ub explains 7.3%. MeCP2 does not titrate to how methylation-skewed a gene is; it reads the ubiquitin mark. This connects the methylation phenotype to the same H2AK119ub Polycomb mechanism that drives the Hi-C compaction arm of the project, and it positions MeCP2 as a chromatin reader rather than a methylation follower.

## Caveats & framing

- **"Preferentially / primarily," not "exclusively."** MeCP2 is canonically a methyl-CpG reader, and the data do not claim it ignores methylation entirely — the modest modC↔MeCP2 coupling is real but context-gated (Section 68: strongest at bivalent promoters rho = 0.46, weakest at unmarked genes rho = 0.07; Section 69: MeCP2 does distinguish 5mC from 5hmC in active/bivalent chromatin). The figure's claim is that CG methylation is **necessary-but-not-sufficient and far weaker than chromatin** for explaining the BAP1-KO redistribution, with K119ub dominant. Frame as "MeCP2 reads chromatin state, primarily H2AK119ub, rather than tracking the CG methylation change" — not as "MeCP2 ignores methylation."

- **MeCP2 is CUT&RUN, never ChIP.** All MeCP2 data in this figure (binding level, differential fold, peak set, status partition) is from MeCP2 CUT&RUN. Labels use "binding" / "signal" / "mark" throughout the script and should do so in the caption. H2AK119ub, H3K27ac, H3K27me3 are the ChIP-derived histone marks; ATAC is accessibility.

- **K119ub sign reconciliation (consistent across the paper).** As in Figure 2, K119ub appears with opposite signs depending on whether it is measured as a *differential fold* or a *baseline absolute* signal — these are compatible:
  - As a *differential* change (this figure: Panel C gene-body K119ub log2FC, Panel D BigWig mean change, Panel E variance of MeCP2 fold; also Section 62 standardized β = +0.199 and Section 71 Spearman rho = +0.187), genes that *gain* K119ub also gain MeCP2 → **positive predictor**.
  - As *baseline constitutive* signal (the TET-impediment model in Figure 3 / Section 24, β ≈ −1.05), genes with high pre-existing K119ub are already compacted/inaccessible → **negative predictor** of further methylation gain.
  - The reconciling statement: *gaining* K119ub recruits/stabilizes MeCP2 and drives the cascade, whereas *constitutively high* K119ub marks already-repressed, compacted regions. Panels C–E all measure the differential (gain) axis, which is the positive one.

- **MeCP2-Up/Down headline n differs by mark (not a discrepancy).** Gene-level classification from `59_quadrant_master.tsv` gives 79 MeCP2-Up / 34 MeCP2-Down genes (FDR < 0.05); the per-mark test n in `60_mecp2_status_stats.tsv` is 78 Up / 35 Down for the K119ub BigWig row, because the `gb_signal_class == "quantifiable"` and per-mark non-NA filters shift one gene each. Panel C reproduces the documented Up = 79 / Down = 34 partition from the master table; Panel D uses the per-mark n (e.g. 78 / 35 for K119ub BigWig). Report the cohort as 79/34 at gene level and the per-mark n where Panel D values are cited.

- **"359 vs 356" cohort for Panel C.** The MeCP2-up-no-methylation candidate cohort is **359 genes** (the Section 65 `mecp2_only` cell); Section 67 computes its statistics on **356** after dropping 3 genes with non-finite K119ub log2FC. Cite the cohort as 359 and the tested n (for OR = 3.15, p = 1.8e-24) as 356. Note also a documented provenance gap: `section_67` reads `65_mecp2_bound_no_methylation_genes.tsv` but the current `section_65` script does not write it — the 359-count is internally consistent across tables (the file has exactly 359 rows and the Fisher table reports `mecp2_only = 359`), so the number is trustworthy, but the generating step is undocumented in the present pipeline.

- **Panel A's embedded-newline read crash (fixed; do not regress).** `62_model_comparison_summary.tsv`'s `label` column embeds a literal `\n`. The shared `read_table_tsv()` uses `fill = FALSE` and will abort on this file. Panel A reads it directly with `fill = TRUE`; if anyone later "tidies" this back to the shared helper, the script breaks. The shared helper must stay unchanged because all 8 figure scripts depend on it.

- **Panel D dispersion is reconstructed, not stored.** The 95% CI bars in Panel D are derived from each row's mean and one-sample t-statistic (SE = |mean / t|), the only dispersion recoverable from the `60_mecp2_status_stats.tsv` summary table. Significance stars come from the BH-adjusted one-sample Wilcoxon q (`wilcox_q < 0.05`), read dynamically per row. The K119ub-at-MeCP2-Down value rendered (mean −0.04, wilcox_q = 0.18, ns) is read from the table, not hardcoded, so it is the correct TSV value even though an earlier self-report mentioned q = 0.32 (a stale note); both are >0.05, so the flat-K119ub punchline holds either way.

- **The 92.6% unexplained variance (Panel E) is a real limitation, not noise to hide.** K119ub explains 7.3% of gene-body MeCP2 fold — dominant relative to the methylation ratio's 0.0%, but a minority of total variance. The honest reading is that K119ub is the *strongest identifiable* determinant of MeCP2 fold, decisively above CG methylation, while most of MeCP2 fold is governed by features beyond these two predictors (binding-level partition in Panel B leaves 74% unexplained for the same reason).

- **All numbers are run-5 canonical (deep-seq, 8 samples, sex covariate).** Statistics derive from the run-5 section TSVs in `plots/visualizations/tables/`; the Section 62/63 BigWig signal caches depend on the external BigWigs in `/Users/zakiralibhai/sdsc/bigwigs/`. FIGURES.md / TODO.md carry no entries for sections 59–71, so these values come directly from the source TSVs (cross-checked against `docs/results/section_59_63_mecp2_mechanistic.md` and `docs/results/section_64_71_global_meth_subcompartment.md`).
