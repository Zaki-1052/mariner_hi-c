# Baseline Non-CG Methylation in Mouse Cerebellar Granule Neurons: Reconciling Your ~0.86% CHH / 0.63% CHG with the Atlas Literature

## TL;DR
- **Your ~0.86% CHH / ~0.63% CHG (P60 cerebellar granule neurons) is fully consistent with the known CGN baseline.** The single best-matched primary value is Mellén et al. 2017 (PNAS), who measured **5mCH = 0.94%** (all non-CG pooled, predominantly mCA) plus 5hmCH = 0.57% in purified *mouse* cerebellar granule cells — essentially identical to your pooled non-CG numbers.
- **Cerebellar granule neurons are confirmed to be the lowest-non-CG-methylation neuron type in the brain** — roughly 2× lower than cortical excitatory neurons (whose mCH/CH reaches ~1.98% by P39, and whose mCA/CA is ~3–4%) and behaving almost like a non-neuronal/glial methylome; they "do not gain" the neuronal non-CG wave.
- **The denominators do not match the cell-atlas "mCA" headline numbers**: your CHH/CHG are pooled-trinucleotide mC/C fractions over non-CG sites, whereas atlas/neuroscience "mCA" is mC at CA dinucleotides only (a subset, with a CA-only denominator that runs ~2.5–3× higher). The defensible comparison is to **global mCH/CH (all non-CG)**, not to mCA/CA.

## Key Findings

**1. The closest defensible CGN-specific number: Mellén et al. 2017, PNAS (doi:10.1073/pnas.1708044114).** In purified adult mouse cerebellar granule cells profiled by MethylC-seq/oxBS/TAB-seq, the paper states verbatim that "the extent of cytosine modification in the CH context (defined as C followed by A, C, or T, ∼1.5%)… non-CG methylation occurs predominantly as 5mCA," and that "5hmCH (0.57%)… [occurs] relative to 5mCH (0.94%)." So **5mCH ≈ 0.94%** and **5hmCH ≈ 0.57%** for a total CH modification of ~1.5%. In the CG context the same cells were "methylcytosine (∼60%)… ∼4.6 times more abundant than hydroxymethylcytosine CG (∼13%)." Mellén's 0.94% 5mCH is the **mCH/CH fraction (all non-CG pooled)** — the same denominator family as your CHH+CHG — and it nearly equals your ~0.86% CHH. This is the number your earlier supplemental search probably missed, because it sits in a MeCP2/hydroxymethylation paper rather than in a cell-atlas table.

**2. Whole-brain single-cell atlas (Liu, Zeng, Zhou et al. 2023, Nature; doi:10.1038/s41586-023-06805-y) — the only Ecker atlas that covers cerebellum.** Across all subclasses the global fractions span **mCG 66.3–85.3% and mCH 0.6–5.6%** (mCH = all non-CG/CpH pooled, global per-cell). Cerebellar **granule** cells sit at the **bottom of the mCH ranking (≈0.6–0.8%, near the floor)**, displayed in Extended Data Fig. 3i,j (ranked by global mCH) and tabulated in the per-subclass pseudo-bulk "mCH/mCG fraction" summary across 274 subclass labels (**Supplementary Table 4**). **Caution on naming:** the high-mCH "CBN Glut" entry (5.4%) is cerebellar *nuclei* glutamatergic neurons, NOT granule cells — the two cerebellar populations are at opposite extremes of the mCH distribution.

**3. The earlier atlas (Liu et al. 2021, Nature; doi:10.1038/s41586-020-03182-8) frames the floor.** It states verbatim: "The global methylation levels range from 0.2% to 7.6% in non-CpG sites and 61.6% to 88.8% in CpG sites (Extended Data Fig. 2b, c)." That 2021 dataset sampled cortex/hippocampus/striatum/pallidum/olfactory only — **no cerebellum** — so the ~0.2% floor is set by non-neuronal cells. It is corroborative-by-analogy: CGNs methylate like that low end. For reference, *hippocampal* dentate gyrus granule cells in that paper were a separate, forebrain population: "DG granule cells were continuously distributed… from low to high global mCH and mCG (Fig. 5d, global mCH fraction 0.5–1.9%, mCG fraction 69–79%)" — not cerebellar.

**4. Cortical reference points confirm CGNs are low.** Luo et al. 2017 (Science) reported genome-wide **mCH 1.3–3.4% in mouse** cortical neuron types (2.8–6.6% in human) — global mCH/CH. Lister et al. 2013 (Science; doi:10.1126/science.1237905) established the adult cortex plateau and the CA-context preference, stating that "only a small fraction of all cytosines throughout the genome are methylated (mCG = 2.9%, mCH = 1.3%, hmC = 0.87%)" — note that is a per-all-cytosine denominator, different again. For a directly comparable cortical mCH/CH anchor, Lavery et al. 2020 (eLife; doi:10.7554/eLife.66909) reported that excitatory-neuron mCH "is absent at birth (<0.1% of all CH sites at P0), and accumulates by postnatal day 39 (1.98% at P39)" — about 2× the CGN value of 0.94%. Cortical excitatory neuron **mCA/CA is ~3–4%**. A CGN mCH of ~0.94% is therefore ~2–4× below cortical excitatory neurons — the literature consistently calls CGNs the lowest non-CG neuron type, and the Dip-C cerebellar atlas (Tan et al.) explicitly notes granule cells "curiously do not gain non-CpG methylation," having a "non-neuronal methylome."

**5. Stroud et al. 2017 (Cell, Greenberg lab) used CGNs but reports patterns, not a clean global CGN mCA percentage.** Their base-resolution WGBS/oxBS maps are of cortex and hippocampus at 1/2/8 weeks; the CGN work in that lineage concerns DNMT3A-dependent mCA *patterning* and MeCP2 readout. Companion MeCP2-dynamics work used Neurod1-Cre;Dnmt3a CGNs and reported that Dnmt3a loss caused ~20% decrease of total 5mC, confirming DNMT3A writes the (low) CGN non-CG complement, but does not provide a clean baseline mCA/CA percentage.

## Details — Making Every Denominator Explicit

This is the crux of your reconciliation problem. The numbers in the literature are **not all the same quantity**:

- **Your CHH (~0.86%) and CHG (~0.63%)**: modified-C / total-C within each pooled trinucleotide context, autosomal, genome-wide. CHH = mC at C-H-H (the nine you listed, e.g. CAC, CAT, CCA…); CHG = mC at C-H-G (CAG, CCG, CTG). Neither is "mCA." Both *contain parts of* mCA: the dominant neuronal context CAC falls in your CHH bucket, and CAG falls in your CHG bucket. A count-weighted pooled **mCH/CH** for your sample would land ~0.75–0.85% (CHH sites vastly outnumber CHG sites, so the pooled value sits very close to your CHH).

- **mCH/CH (all non-CG)**: the quantity Mellén (0.94% for CGNs), Luo (1.3–3.4% cortex), Lavery (1.98% cortical excitatory at P39), and the atlases (global mCH) report. **This is your correct comparison target.** Your pooled non-CG ≈ Mellén's 0.94% CGN value → consistent.

- **mCA/CA (CA dinucleotide only)**: the headline number in most neuroscience/MeCP2 papers (cortical excitatory ~3–4%, PV/SST higher). Because CA is the preferentially methylated context, mCA/CA runs ~2.5–3× higher than the pooled mCH/CH in the same cells. **Do not compare your 0.86% CHH directly to a 3–5% mCA number** — different numerator subset and different denominator. If you need an apples-to-apples mCA/CA for your sample, recompute restricting to CA dinucleotides (CAA+CAC+CAT from your CHH, plus CAG from your CHG) over a CA-site denominator; expect it to be modestly higher than 0.86%.

- **mCAC-weighted / gene-body mCA**: atlas marker-gene and MeCP2 analyses often use gene-body mCA or specifically the CAC subcontext (the strongest DNMT3A target). These are spatially restricted measures, not genome-wide global, and are not comparable to your global autosomal number.

- **Global per-cell fraction**: single-cell atlas values (Liu 2021/2023) are per-cell mC/C over covered cytosines, pooled non-CG — comparable to your pooled mCH, modulo single-cell sparsity.

**Bulk-tissue vs purified-CGN interpretation (both flagged, as requested):**

The usual rule — "bulk brain tissue dilutes neuronal mCH because glia have near-zero non-CG" — **largely collapses in the cerebellum**, and this is what makes your number robust either way:

- *If your sample is purified/sorted CGNs:* expected mCH/CH ≈ 0.9–1% (Mellén). Your ~0.86% CHH matches directly.
- *If your sample is bulk cerebellum:* CGNs are by far the most abundant cell (the most numerous neuron in the brain), and crucially **CGNs themselves carry a near-glial-low non-CG load**, so the glial-dilution gap is small. Bulk cerebellum would read only slightly below purified CGN — still ~0.6–0.9% pooled mCH. Your number is consistent with this too.

In other words, unlike cortex (where bulk ≪ purified neuron because cortical neurons are high-mCH and glia drag the average down), cerebellum gives nearly the same low non-CG readout whether or not you purify — because the dominant neuron is intrinsically low. Your ~0.86% CHH does **not** require invoking a strong dilution artifact; it is the expected biological baseline.

**One technical caveat to check:** ~0.6–0.9% non-CG can in principle be inflated by incomplete bisulfite/enzymatic conversion. The atlases use mCCC (CpCpC) < 3% as the non-conversion ceiling; careful CGN studies (Mellén) put the *true* CGN mCH near 0.94%. Your values are in the genuine-signal range, but confirm your lambda/unmethylated-spike-in CHH non-conversion rate is well below your 0.86% to be certain the signal is biological and not residual unconverted C.

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt Mellén et al. 2017 (PNAS, 5mCH = 0.94% in mouse CGNs) as your primary benchmark.** It is the cleanest purified-CGN, correct-denominator (mCH/CH) value and it matches your data. Cite it as your headline consistency check.
2. **Report your comparison as mCH/CH, not mCA.** State explicitly that your CHH+CHG pool ≈ literature mCH/CH ≈ 0.9%, and that this is distinct from the mCA/CA values (~3–4% in cortical excitatory neurons) seen in atlas headline figures. This single clarification resolves your "wasn't quite what I wanted" supplemental confusion.
3. **For the atlas number you wanted, pull Liu et al. 2023 Supplementary Table 4 (per-subclass pseudo-bulk mCH/mCG summary) or read Extended Data Fig. 3j**, and look specifically for the "CB granule"/cerebellar granule subclass (expect ~0.6–0.8% global mCH). Use the interactive browser at the Salk neomorph/mousebrain portal to read the exact decimal. **Explicitly avoid the "CBN Glut" row (5.4%)** — that is cerebellar nuclei, not granule cells.
4. **If a reviewer wants mCA/CA specifically, recompute it** from your per-context tables restricted to CA dinucleotides, so you can state both your pooled mCH (~0.86% CHH) and a true mCA/CA for your sample.
5. **Verify conversion efficiency** (lambda spike-in CHH/CHG non-conversion) to confirm the ~0.86% is biological. Benchmark/threshold that would change the interpretation: if your spike-in non-conversion is ≳0.5–0.8%, a meaningful fraction of your signal could be artifact and the comparison to Mellén weakens.

**What would change these conclusions:** a measured mCA/CA in your sample far above ~3% would indicate either a contaminating high-mCH neuron population (e.g., cerebellar nuclei neurons, Purkinje, molecular-layer interneurons) or a labeling/region error; a value at/below ~0.5% with high conversion efficiency would push toward a glia-dominated or immature (pre-mCH-accumulation) sample.

## Caveats
- The exact per-subclass cerebellar-granule global mCH from Liu et al. 2023 lives in Extended Data Fig. 3i,j and Supplementary Table 4 (figure/table data), not in the running text; the ~0.6–0.8% estimate is read off the ranking and the stated 0.6% global mCH floor, pending your confirming the precise decimal in the supplement.
- Mellén's 0.94% is mCH (5mC at non-CG); it co-occurs with ~0.57% hmCH in CGNs. Standard bisulfite/enzymatic conversion does **not** distinguish 5mC from 5hmC, so a WGBS/EM-seq "non-CG methylation" readout in CGNs may include a hydroxymethyl component. This is a CGN-specific subtlety (cerebellum is unusually hmC-rich) and could make a bisulfite-based pooled non-CG number slightly *higher* than the true 5mC-only value.
- Luo 2017 and the 2021 atlas did not sample cerebellum; their values are cortical/forebrain references, not CGN measurements. Lavery 2020's 1.98% is cortical excitatory neuron mCH/CH (the directly comparable cortical anchor).
- "mCA is ~60% of mCH" is the commonly cited context split; the precise CA fraction varies by cell type, so converting your pooled CHH into an exact mCA requires your own per-context counts rather than a fixed multiplier.
- Stroud 2017's base-resolution WGBS maps are cortex/hippocampus; treat that paper as the mechanism (DNMT3A→mCA→MeCP2) reference, not as a source of a CGN global mCA percentage.
