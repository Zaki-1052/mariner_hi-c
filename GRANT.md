SPECIFIC AIMS
Precise temporal control of gene expression, operating at the level of both chromatin (histone modification) and DNA methylation, is required for neurons to establish and maintain their identity throughout their maturation. Mutations affecting Polycomb Repressive Complex 1 (PRC1) and its deubiquitinase BAP1 cause syndromic neurodevelopmental disorders (NDDs) marked by developmental delay, seizures, and intellectual disability1–3—phenotypes overlapping the broader family of inherited disorders that result from mutation in chromatin regulators4,5. How disruption of histone-modifying enzymes produces such pervasive neurodevelopmental dysfunction, and the extent to which they regulate the epigenome remain largely unknown.
Our lab discovered that histone H2A ubiquitination (H2AK119ub, hereafter H2Aub), deposited by PRC1, is dynamically redistributed away from silenced loci toward active enhancers and promoters during cerebellar neurodevelopment, overturning the textbook view that Polycomb complexes act only in a repressed context6. To determine how loss of the deubiquitinase BAP1 perturbs these processes, we generated two complementary conditional Bap1 mutant mouse models7. In Bap1f/f,Math1-cre mice8 with Bap1 deletion in cerebellar granule neurons, CUT&RUN and transcriptomic profiling revealed that euchromatin and heterochromatin are weakened by Bap1 loss: euchromatic loci gain H2Aub and PRC2-dependent H3K27me3 and lose the activating mark H3K27ac, becoming repressed. Heterochromatic loci, in contrast, show the reciprocal pattern and become overexpressed. In a second conditional mutant, Bap1f/f,Emx1-Cre mice, which delete Bap1 in forebrain excitatory neurons9, mutants recapitulate the developmental delay, seizures, and behavioral abnormalities of patients with BAP1 mutations. These mutants serve as our platform for the molecular and functional rescue experiments, respectively, described below.
Because H2Aub recruits the DNA methylation writer DNMT3A10–12, we asked whether BAP1 loss remodels the neuronal methylome. Using Biomodal’s evoC assay to simultaneously profile repressive 5-methylcytosine (5mC) and activating 5-hydroxymethylcytosine (5hmC)13, we found that Bap1 mutant cerebellar granule neurons show reciprocal methylation changes that track with changes in histone modifications. Indeed, euchromatic loci gained 5mC and lost 5hmC, while heterochromatic loci showed the opposite pattern. Exogenously expressed and purified GST-tagged MeCP2 (the reader of methylated DNA)14,15, redistributed in parallel at loci possessing non-CG methylation, a DNMT3A-dependent, neuronally enriched repressive modification16,17. These findings support a model in which H2Aub redistribution alters DNMT3A recruitment, redirecting methylation into loci that gain H2Aub. This altered methylation landscape redirects binding of MeCP2, which recruits the NCoR corepressor complex18 and its associated histone deacetylase to strip H3K27ac from these loci. This cascade (H2Aub → DNMT3A recruitment → DNA methylation → MeCP2 → NCoR/HDAC3 → H3K27ac loss) resolves a central conundrum in our data: how loss of the single histone ubiquitin mark produces such severe, widespread changes in a seemingly unrelated modification H3K27ac. Because BAP1 loss perturbs H2Aub bidirectionally, our system offers an unusually clean test of this cascade, pursued in the following aims.
Aim 1: Establish the sequence by which H2Aub, DNA methylation and H3K27ac change after BAP1 loss, and determine their dependence on DNMT3A. In the cerebellum of Bap1f/f,Math1-cre mice we will map H2Aub, H3K27ac, MeCP2 (CUT&RUN), 5mC/5hmC (evoC) and non-CG methylation (whole-genome bisulfite sequencing, WGBS) across a developmental time course (P6, P12, P22, adult). Redistribution of H2Aub should occur first, with methylation and H3K27ac changes following. In parallel, we will cross a floxed Dnmt3a allele19 onto this Bap1 background to generate Bap1f/f,Dnmt3af/+,Math1-cre compound mutants, testing whether reduced DNMT3A corrects methylation and H3K27ac changes to rescue gene expression.
Aim 2: Determine whether BAP1 catalytic-domain mutations remodel DNA methylation through H2Aub in human neurons. We are introducing two patient mutations into a doxycycline-inducible NGN2 human iPSC line to generate isogenic excitatory neurons. We will profile H2Aub, H3K27ac, and H3K27me3 (CUT&RUN) alongside transcriptomics and methylation (evoC, WGBS, MeCP2-GST CUT&RUN), extending the differentiation period to capture the slow accrual of non-CG methylation. Concordant histone and methylation changes will establish that BAP1 controls DNA methylation through H2Aub in a human, disease-relevant model.
Aim 3: Ascertain whether restoring non-CG methylation rescues seizures caused by BAP1 loss. Bap1f/f,Emx1-Cre mice with BAP1 loss in excitatory neurons of the forebrain experience frequent seizures. Mice with heterozygous global loss of Dnmt3a (i.e. Dnmt3a+/-) exhibit ~50% reduction in non-CG methylation, leading to developmental delay and other behavioral abnormalities but, importantly, not seizures20. To determine whether normalizing non-CG hypermethylation can correct the neurophysiologic consequences of Bap1 loss, we will generate Bap1f/f,Dnmt3af/+,Emx1-cre compound mutants before performing in vivo electroencephalography and patch clamp recordings from acute hippocampal slices. This epistasis design directly tests whether perturbation of non-CG methylation underlies circuit dysfunction, and whether DNMT3A is a druggable target through which the neurological consequences of perturbing H2Aub could be reversed.

RESEARCH STRATEGY
This proposal will establish the mechanistic link between the histone modifications and DNA methylation in neurons, uniting major mechanism of epigenetic regulation. We propose to understand how loss of BAP1 ultimately impacts H3K27ac through a ubiquitin-DNMT3A-MeCP2-NCoR axis. We will define the neurodevelopmental window in which this cascade operates and determine whether DNMT3A is candidate therapeutic targets for BAP1-related neurodevelopmental disorders. 
A. SIGNIFICANCE
Epigenetic control of neurodevelopment and its disruption in disease. Building a functional nervous system requires that developing neurons execute precise, cell type- and stage-specific gene expression programs as they exit the cell cycle, differentiate and become functionally integrated into circuits. This process relies primarily upon cell-intrinsic processes of epigenetic regulation. Principal among these are chemical modifications to DNA and histone proteins that establish transcriptionally permissive euchromatin and repressive heterochromatin states at individual loci. The centrality of these processes for neurodevelopment is underscored by inherited neurodevelopmental disorders (NDD), as epigenetic regulators rank among the largest classes of genes implicated in inherited forms of NDD4,5,21,22. Furthermore, syndromes like Kabuki (KMT2D), Coffin-Siris (SWI/SNF), Cornelia de Lange (NIPBL/cohesin), Rett (MECP2), and Tatton-Brown-Rahman (DNMT3A), share striking overlapping features, including intellectual disability, seizures and autism23–27. This phenotypic convergence suggests that these disorders disrupt an underlying mechanism that perturbs neuronal gene regulation. Therefore, defining the individual parts that contribute to this shared logic, and how they interact with one another, informs our understanding of this entire disease class.
BAP1-mediated control of a multifaceted chromatin regulatory program in neurons. BAP1 is the catalytic subunit of the Polycomb Repressive Deubiquitinase (PR-DUB) complex28. The principal function of BAP1’s enzymatic activity is removal of H2A ubiquitinated at lysine 119 (H2AK119ub1, hereafter H2Aub) (Fig. 1A). H2Aub is initially deposited by the ubiquitin ligase subunits of non-canonical/variant Polycomb Repressive Complex 1 (PRC1)29,30, before BAP1 acts to constrain H2Aub. As such, loss of BAP1 in embryonic stem cells (ESCs) causes H2Aub to spread broadly31,32. An essential PRC1 effector is Polycomb Repressive Complex 2 (PRC2), which deposits H3K27me3, the hallmark of gene repression in facultative heterochromatin33. PRC1 and PRC2 engage in a mutual read-write relationship—PRC2 is recruited and activated by H2Aub, while PRC1 is recruited to H3K27me3-marked chromatin by Cbx734–36. However, our lab recently discovered that neuronal H2Aub often appears in the absence of H3K27me3, and in this context H2Aub is much more developmentally dynamic than where H2Aub and H3K27me3 are colocalized6. We also discovered that neuronal H2Aub appears within active enhancers and promoters (i.e. euchromatic loci), where it becomes enriched during neurodevelopment while, remarkably, undergoing simultaneous depletion from facultative heterochromatin6. 
To understand the function of neuronal H2Aub in these distinct chromatin contexts, we generated the first brain-specific mouse mutants affecting Bap1. To our surprise, neuronal BAP1 loss altered multiple histone modifications beyond H2Aub within both heterochromatin and euchromatin. Furthermore, the consequences of Bap1 loss differed depending upon the chromatin context¬¬¬—euchromatin lost activating histone modifications while gaining repressive modifications, whereas heterochromatin lost repressive modifications while gaining activating modifications, effectively homogenizing chromatin states. This leads inappropriate reducing expression of euchromatic genes while heterochromatic loci escape repression. This is one the few instances in which a single protein is required to form both heterochromatin and euchromatin. 
Our observations broaden the understanding of the role of Bap1 in the brain, and the regulation of euchromatin by ubiquitin, which turns out to be as important as H2Aub’s role in silencing. Remarkably, in both forms of chromatin, the active mark H3K27ac moves in opposition to H2Aub (euchromatin loses the former and gains the latter, while heterochromatin gains the former and loses the latter). But the mechanistic intermediaries lying between BAP1’s direct target H2Aub and H3K27ac is a central gap this proposal is designed to address. If true, our hypothesis that this intermediary is DNA methylation (which our preliminary data show is markedly perturbed by BAP1 loss) would place Bap1 and its substrate H2Aub at the heart of a multifaceted gene regulatory program operating at multiple levels within both euchromatin and heterochromatin.  
Modeling BAP1 in neurodevelopmental disorders. BAP1 has long been studied as a tumor suppressor gene: somatic loss of function mutations are associated with multiple malignancies37–39, and germline loss-of-function variants cause the BAP1 tumor predisposition syndrome. However, is now established that a distinct set of germline BAP1 mutations underlie the neurodevelopmental disease Küry-Isidor syndrome, which includes intellectual disability and seizures1. To develop the first human model of BAP1 mutation, we will introduce patient mutations that congregate in the enzymatic UCH (ubiquitin-specific hydrolase) domain into human embryonic stem cells (ESCs) and differentiate them into induced neurons. This model will allow us to connect alterations in H2Aub to changes in DNA methylation and ultimately the neuronal transcriptome, which remain entirely undefined and central to the pathology of patients. These neuronal models also allow us to ask a critical question in molecular biology, which relates to the structural versus strictly enzymatic roles of BAP1 in chromatin regulation. BAP1 interact with many different chromatin regulatory complexes40,41, and it is unclear whether these the molecular phenotypes we observe result from loss of its enzymatic activity rather than its ability to interact with binding partners and potentially recruit them to chromatin. The mouse mutant deletes BAP1 entirely, preventing inference of which phenotypes result from loss of BAP1’s enzymatic activity versus its structural role.  
Our model’s broader significance. Our central hypothesis is BAP1 loss triggers a defined molecular cascade: redistribution of H2Aub into euchromatin (and away from heterochromatin) alters genomic recruitment of the de novo methyltransferase DNMT3A, which changes patterns of non-CG methylation; this in turn redirects binding of the DNA methylation reader MeCP2, which recruits the NCoR corepressor complex and its associated histone deacetylase HDAC3 to remove H3K27ac18,42,43. This would account for our observation that H3K27ac decreases in euchromatin and increases within heterochromatin, in opposition to changes in H2Aub. While there is strong preliminary evidence from several different labs for each step in this cascade, ours is the first proposal to tie them together into a single model in which H2Aub drives gene silencing at multiple levels. In particular, the recruitment of DNMT3A by H2Aub has been defined biochemically but not functionally, as our proposal will. We will also test the prediction that H2Aub is the upstream regulator of DNMT3A-dependent non-CG methylation (methyl-CH). Neuronally enriched methyl-CH has garnered intense interest among molecular neuroscientists for its potential to tune neuronal gene expression16,44 and define different neuronal sub-types45. These experiments thus carry significance well beyond the BAP1 neurodevelopmental syndrome by placing it mechanistically alongside two of the best-studied neurodevelopmental disorders that act through a similar machinery—Rett syndrome (MECP2) and Tatton-Brown-Rahman syndrome (DNMT3A), for which methylation-dependent, MeCP2-mediated repression is similarly central18,46. Establishing this connection would be the first direct evidence that histone ubiquitination and reading DNA methylation function as a single regulatory pathway in neurons, rather than three independently studied layers, reframing how a broader class of overlapping chromatin-based neurodevelopmental syndromes is understood. Because the terminal step of the proposed cascade—NCoR-associated HDAC activity — is already pharmacologically tractable, defining this pathway also opens a concrete, testable path toward pharmacologic intervention in BAP1 syndrome and in mechanistically related disorders, a possibility that has not previously existed for any Polycomb-related disease gene.
B. INNOVATION
DNA methylation as an instructed output of histone ubiquitination. H2Aub and DNA methylation have been conventionally studied separately. However, multiple recent studies have shown a biochemical interaction between DNMT3A and H2Aub10–12. Nevertheless, we have barely scratched the surface of understanding the functional consequences of this interaction, particularly in the brain. 
The major form of DNA methylation in most cells is 5-methylcytosine (5mC). 5mC appears almost entirely at CG dinucleotides, being deposited by DNMT1 during S phase47,48. However, after neurons exit the cell cycle, 5mC is deposited de novo by DNMT3A outside of the typical CG context, mainly at CA dinucleotides but also at CT and CC (collectively termed methyl-CH). CG is a palindrome, therefore methyl-CG is symmetric across both DNA strands. The fact that the other dinucleotides are non-palindromic adds another layer of regulation because 5mC occurs on the sense or anti-sense strand but not both. Because methyl-CH is abundant in neurons but rare in other cell types, its epigenetic functions have garnered significant attention since its discovery49. The current model is that DNMT3A deposits methyl-CH following recruitment by the histone modifications H3K36me2 and H3K36me350,51. This proposal adds another regulatory layer with strong support from preliminary data: that in postmitotic neurons, methyl-CG and methyl-CH are directly regulated by H2Aub. This reframing yields a directional, falsifiable prediction pursued in Aim 1: Bap1-dependent changes in DNA methylation should require, and follow, H2Aub redistribution rather than arise independently of it. This hierarchy that has not been tested for BAP1 or any other Polycomb related protein.
A single mechanistic cascade unifying three neurodevelopmental disease genes. Küry-Isidor (BAP1), Tatton-Brown-Rahman (DNMT3A), and Rett  (MECP2) syndromes are presently grouped only by their overlapping symptoms. One of this proposal's conceptual advances is to specify the molecular order of operations connecting them: H2Aub redistribution redirects DNMT3A, which redirects CG and non-CG methylation, which redirects binding of MeCP2 (which avidly binds to both forms of DNA methylation52), which recruits the NCoR/HDAC18,42 complex to erase H3K27ac. Framed this way, Küry-Isidor is not merely phenotypically adjacent to Rett and Tatton-Brown-Rahman syndromes but mechanistically upstream of them, predicting a specific pathogenic sequence that unites histone ubiquitination and neuronal DNA methylation.
Resolving a chromatin paradox by inserting DNA methylation as the essential intermediate. A central puzzle in our preliminary data is that loss of a deubiquitinase depletes H3K27ac, a euchromatic mark without any obvious relation to H2Aub. No existing model can explain account for the scale and locus-selectivity of the effects on H3K27ac we observe. We resolve this paradox by inserting an intervening step: aberrant DNA methylation, not H2Aub or H3K27me3 per se, redirects MeCP2 and its associated HDAC activity to strip H3K27ac. This is a mechanistically distinct explanation from anything previously proposed for BAP1 or any other PRC1 component, and it is testable because each step of the cascade—H2Aub, DNMT3A, DNA methylation, MeCP2 binding and H3K27ac—can be independently measured.
Bidirectional H2Aub perturbation provides an internally controlled test. Because BAP1 loss redistributes H2Aub in both directions at once (gain within euchromatin, depletion within heterochromatin) our system predicts reciprocal changes in DNA methylation at specific loci. This presents a unifying model in which BAP1 controls methylation state within these compartments to control a key aspect of their identities.
Testing whether one node of the cascade is sufficient to modify neurological phenotypes. Prior work on Polycomb-related disease genes has documented chromatin abnormalities without testing whether any single downstream change is sufficient to modify any specific phenotype. The epistasis testing in Aims 1 will assess the interaction between H2Aub and DNA methylation via generation of double mutants with Dnmt3a to titrate non-CG methylation and assess rescue of downstream molecular phenotypes. In Aim 3, we utilize seizures as an unconfounded readout in vivo (BAP1 loss in forebrain excitatory mutants causes seizures, heterozygous Dnmt3a knockout does not). Generating double mutants will determine whether seizures are causally required for a key functional neurological consequence.
Base-resolution discrimination of 5-methylcytosine (5mC) from 5-hydroxymethylcytosine (5hmC), rather than their conflation into a single signal. Standard bisulfite sequencing cannot distinguish 5-methylcytosine from 5-hydroxymethylcytosine, as both marks are protected from conversion and are read identically as unconverted cytosine. This is a critical limitation for our model, because 5hmC is not simply an inert intermediate but a dynamically regulated mark with its own distinct genomic distribution, being enriched at expressed gene bodies and active enhancers and particularly abundant in neurons53,54. Use of Biomodal’s six-base evoC assay resolves this ambiguity through clever biochemistry in which 5mC and 5hmC are converted into distinct identifiable two-base sequencing code using a combination of TET2 oxidation, β-glucosyltransferase protection, and strand-specific enzymatic copying13. This remarkable technical advance has allowed us track 5mC and 5hmC as two independent readouts at single-base resolution from the same DNA molecule in a closed experimental system. This has allowed us to determine that BAP1 loss causes opposing effects on 5mC and 5hmC within both euchromatin and heterochromatin.
An affinity-based CUT&RUN readout of non-CG methylation that renders our detailed developmental time course tractable. In mature neurons, non-CG methylation accumulates postnatally and becomes one of MeCP2’s dominant genomic binding substrates44,55. We found that the new CUTANA reagent from EpiCypher, in which a recombinantly expressed GST-tagged version of the MeCP2 methyl-binding domain (hereafter MeCP2-GST) serves as the primary affinity reagent in a native CUT&RUN reaction, effectively reads out non-CG methylation, being highly correlated to our own whole-genome bisulfite sequencing data from the mouse cerebellum, as well as other published datasets56. Remarkably, we observe that euchromatic loci which gain H2Aub also gain MeCP2-GST binding, whereas heterochromatic loci that lose H2Aub also lose MeCP2-GST in proportion. This is a technical advance in three respects. First, it repurposes a methyl-binding domain as the affinity reagent in a native CUT&RUN reaction, so the assay reports the methylation that MeCP2 actually engages, which is the quantity our cascade predicts should change. Second, it makes the experiments in this proposal feasible. Base-resolution mCH profiling requires very deep sequencing because non-CG methylation is sparse; at the depth needed for confident mCH calls, profiling four developmental timepoints across the genotypes in Aims 1.1 and 1.4 by sequencing alone would be costly. MeCP2-GST CUT&RUN delivers a locus-resolved readout at a small fraction of that cost and input, allowing us to survey the full time course and every compound-mutant genotype, then apply whole-genome bisulfite sequencing selectively where base-resolution is required. Third, it provides an orthogonal measurement: DNA methylation inferred from cytosine conversion versus DNA methylation inferred from MeCP2 occupancy are complementary and wholly distinct methods, so concordance between them provides far stronger evidence than either could alone. 
C. APPROACH 
C1. PRELIMINARY STUDIES
Experimental neurodevelopmental systems. We have employed two complementary conditional Bap1 mouse mutants tailored to the goals of determining the molecular functions of BAP1 and the behavioral consequences BAP1 loss. Bap1f/f,Math1-cre mice allow us to examine the consequences of BAP1 loss within the abundant and relatively homogeneous population of granule neurons for the deep molecular time-course in Aim 1. For the molecular experiments, we input nuclei isolated from the cerebellum by mechanical dissociation in detergent-containing buffers, and approach that recovers small granule neuron nuclei by lysing much larger nuclei of non-mutant Purkinje neurons6. Bap1f/f,Emx1-cre mice, with BAP1 loss in forebrain excitatory neurons recapitulate patient-relevant seizure phenotype, making these ideal for evaluating the potential for functional rescue via restoration of DNA methylation levels. Finally, the induced neurons in Aim 2 represent a non-animal model for evaluating the effects on patient-specific BAP1 mutation on DNA methylation. Should the alternative approaches in Aim 2 be invoked, mouse ESC-derived neurons would be used alongside or the human lines.
Preliminary Studies: BAP1 loss redistributes H2Aub away from facultative heterochromatin and into euchromatin. BAP1 is the catalytic subunit of the PR-DUB complex, which removes H2Aub deposited by the Polycomb Repressive Complex 1 (Fig. 1A). Before embarking upon an exploration of the molecular functions of BAP1, we first needed to establish its localization in neurons. This step was essential because studies in melanoma cells showed that BAP1 deubiquitinates cytoplasmic substrates57 in addition to H2Aub. Our attempts to detect BAP1 in mouse neurons by immunostaining were unsuccessful. Therefore, we examined human ocular specimens enucleated for uveal melanoma and subjected to immunohistochemical detection of BAP1, which is prognostically significant37. In the coincidentally stained macula of the neurosensory retina, BAP1 labeled nuclei within all the neuronal layers, but was absent from the plexiform layers containing neurites and synapses (Fig. 1B). Therefore, BAP1 localizes to nuclei of diverse neurons (projecting retinal ganglion cells, interneurons in the inner nuclear layer and highly specialized photoreceptors) without any discernible cytoplasmic signal. 
To define BAP1’s role in postmitotic neurons, we deleted BAP1 in progenitors of cerebellar granule neurons of Bap1f/f,Math1-cre8, before profiling H2Aub in the adult cerebellum using the high-resolution/low background next generation sequencing method CUT&RUN (Cleavage Under Targets and Release Using Nuclease)58,59. Rather than a uniform increase, as might be predicted from loss of a deubiquitinating enzyme, and as was observed in Bap1 mutant embryonic stem cells, we observed a striking redistribution: 10,068 H2Aub peak regions (25%) gained H2Aub while 1,632 (4%) exhibited H2Aub reduction (Fig. 1C). We compared loci exhibiting dysregulated H2Aub with the Cistrome repository, which compares user-defined regions of interest with thousands of chromatin mapping datasets to report the GIGGLE score (Genomics Search Engine for Large-Scale Integrated Genome), a statistical measure of similarity60,61. These comparisons showed that regions where H2Aub was gained resembled euchromatic marks (H3K4me1, H2A.Z-ac, H3K27ac), with the strongest concordance in cerebellum-derived datasets, whereas depleted regions bore greatest similarity to H3K27me3 (Fig. 1D,1E). BAP1 therefore does not simply oppose PRC1; it bidirectionally perturbs H2Aub to enforce the partitioning of H2Aub between active and repressed compartments.
Preliminary Studies: Redistributed H2Aub antagonizes acetylation of active enhancers and promoters. CUT&RUN detection of H3K27ac in BAP1-deficient neurons demonstrated loci such as the intergenic enhancers upstream of the Rims1 gene in which increases in H2Aub occurred in concert with decreases in the euchromatin-associated modification H3K27ac (Fig. 2A). In contrast, heterochromatic loci like Myc exhibited a paradoxical reduction in H2Aub that was accompanied by reduction of the repressive modification H3K27me3 (Fig. 2B). Remarkably, this locus and other heterochromatic loci exhibited simultaneous increases in H3K27ac and promoter-associated H3K4me3. Genome-wide differential analysis demonstrated that decreases in H3K27ac predominated (Fig. 2C), and that H2Aub and H3K27ac marks change in opposite directions at individual loci (Fig. 2D). These observations indicate a bidirectional interference with the maintenance of chromatin state, while also revealing a previously unrecognized inverse relationship between H2Aub and H3K27ac in neurons.
Preliminary Studies: Transcriptomic dysregulation converges on synaptic and other neuronal functions. RNA-seq analysis of nuclei isolated from the developing (age P12) and adult cerebellum demonstrated segregation by age and genotype in PCA (Fig. 3A). The transcriptome of the mutant cerebellum contained 1,147 downregulated and 590 upregulated transcripts (Fig. 3B). As predicted, significantly downregulated transcripts tended to gain repressive and lose activating histone modifications, whereas upregulation transcripts exhibited changes in the opposite direction (Fig. 3C). GO enrichment was dominated by synapse assembly, postsynaptic density, neurotransmitter secretion, and ion channel activity (Fig. 3D), implicating regulation of synaptic function as the principal downstream target of BAP1-mediated gene regulation. 
C2. EXPERIMENTAL DESIGN AND RESEARCH METHODS
Rigor and Reproducibility. The genome-wide chromatin mapping experiments are, by design, simple two-condition comparisons that are well suited to rigorous execution in mice. In every mouse experiment, mutants are compared to Cre-negative or, given the recessive nature of Bap1 loss, flox-negative littermate controls generated from the same crosses and housed together, so that genotype is the only systematic variable. Both sexes are included at each developmental timepoint, with sex incorporated as a covariate in all differential models and tested explicitly for genotype-by-sex interaction. Sample sizes follow our completed preliminary studies, in which n=6 biological replicates per genotype yielded well-powered CUT&RUN differential analyses and n=7 for RNA-seq. Analyses use established, publicly available pipelines already validated in our hands—SEACR for peak calling of CUT&RUN data62, DiffBind for assessment of differential binding63, DESeq2 for transcript abundance64 with statistical thresholds (FDR<0.05) and spike-in normalization strategies specified in advance. All antibodies and CUT&RUN affinity reagents are validated for CUT&RUN and show obvious genotype differences, which are highly unlikely for non-specific reagents. We will sequence all experiments at the UCSD Institutive for Genomic Medicine core on the Illumina NovaSeqX platform, with automated quality control performed in real time via RTA4, which assigns per-base quality scores and flags runs falling below standard Q30 thresholds. FastQC and MultiQC65 are run automatically on completed data, generating standardized quality reports (per-base quality, adapter content, duplication rates) for review prior to downstream analysis. Each CUT&RUN experiment will be sequenced to a minimum of 30 million reads. H2Aub, which is broadly distributed, will receive 50 million reads. We will deposit sequencing data in GEO and release the accession code publicly.
For methylation studies (evoC and WGBS) we will include 4 samples per genotype at each timepoint tested, with equal males and females. To buffer biological variability, we will adopt an approach that we used to generate our preliminary data, which included equimolar amounts of DNA from three biological replicates within each sample. Therefore, a total of 24 animals go into each methylation experiment. Analyses of DNA methylation also employ established, publicly available pipelines already validated in our hands. evoC reads will be processed through the Biomodal DUET v1.5.0 pipeline13. Their XPLR performs binomial logistic regression for differential methylation and hydroxymethylation of CG dinucleotides, with the option to specify several covariates. For WGBS, reads will be aligned with the bwa-meth tool and methylation called with MethylDackel, and the edgeR66 quasi-likelihood F-test67 for non-CG differential methylation—with statistical thresholds (FDR<0.05) and spike-in normalization (per-sample lambda phage conversion rate as a model covariate) specified in advance. Each EvoC and WGBS replicate will be sequenced to a minimum depth of 500 million reads for >30x coverage. 
The human iPSC experiments require additional safeguards because CRISPR editing can introduce off-target mutations that segregate with a single clone rather than with the intended genotype. We will therefore analyze three independently derived clones for each patient BAP1 mutation and three independently edited isogenic wild-type clones that have passed through the identical editing and single-cell cloning process. Clone is treated as the unit of biological replication, and only phenotypes reproduced across all three independent clones per genotype will be interpreted as true phenotypes. Predicted off-target sites will be sequenced in each clone, and karyotype and pluripotency markers confirmed before inducing differentiation.
C3. SPECIFIC AIMS
Aim 1: Establish the sequence by which H2Aub, DNA methylation and H3K27ac change and after Bap1 loss, and determine their dependence on DNMT3A.
Aim 1 rationale. Our preliminary data establish that BAP1 loss redistributes H2Aub away from heterochromatin toward euchromatin in postmitotic granule neurons. The correlation between BAP1-dependent H2Aub changes and differences in the binding of MeCP2-GST (reporting DNMT3A-dependent non-CG methylation). If DNA methylation lies downstream of H2Aub, as proposed in our model, then DNA methylation changes should lie temporally and spatially downstream of H2Aub redistribution. This aim tests that directional prediction directly, using the Bap1f/f,Math1-cre model because it isolates a single, relatively homogeneous postmitotic neuronal population, allowing us to match epigenomic and transcriptomic datasets to a defined molecular time course without the cellular heterogeneity confound. Establishing both the sequence and the genetic requirement for DNMT3A here is the foundation for Aims 2 and 3: Aim 2 asks whether the same hierarchy holds in human neurons carrying disease-causing BAP1 mutations; Aim 3 asks whether the DNA methylation steps identified here are functionally required for a defined neurological phenotype.
Aim 1 preliminary data: BAP1 loss remodels the neuronal DNA methylome differently according to chromatin compartment. Using the biomodal duet evoC assay, which resolves 5-methylcytosine (5mC) and 5-hydroxymethylcytosine (5hmC) from the same DNA sample13, we profiled granule neuron nuclei from adult Bap1f/f,Math1-cre mice and littermate controls. Across protein-coding genes, Bap1 loss shifted both modifications, but in opposite directions. The predominant effect was gain of 5mC with loss of 5hmC (Fig. 4A, 4B). Comparing the two marks directly at genes with significant genotype difference in both modifications, we found that 79% gained 5mC while simultaneously losing 5hmC (Fig. 4C). Most of the remaining genes showed the converse. Stratifying loci by chromatin state, as defined by our own CUT&RUN data, revealed that the direction of change was not uniform across the genome but is instead dictated by the compartment in which a locus resides (Fig. 4D). Euchromatic loci—active promoters and enhancers—almost invariably gained repressive 5mC and lost activity-associated 5hmC, while Polycomb-repressed loci showed the reciprocal pattern; bivalent promoters were split between the two behaviors, as expected given their mixed character. Critically, these are the same compartments in which H2Aub moves opposite directions: euchromatic loci that gain H2Aub in mutant neurons also acquire repressive methylation, while Polycomb loci that lose H2Aub lose it. Therefore, the blurring of euchromatin and heterochromatin is therefore mirrored in the DNA methylome.
Aim 1 preliminary data: Remodeling 5mC and 5hmC accompanies transcriptional dysregulation at Syt1. Genome-wide changes are exemplified by Syt1. In mutant granule neurons, H2Aub and traditional 5-methyl-CG accumulate across the Syt1 gene body while 5hmC is depleted (Fig. 5B). Correspondingly, the Syt1 transcript declines by roughly five-fold (Fig. 5B). Emblematic of the synaptic vesicle machinery that is broadly dysregulated by BAP1 loss, Syt1 is among the most abundant transcripts in these neurons, representing an example of the link between histone modification and DNA methylation defects. Together, these data establish that BAP1 loss remodels the neuronal methylome in a manner that tracks H2Aub redistribution. What they cannot establish is whether methylation changes follow H2Aub redistribution or arise in parallel with it. 
Aim 1 preliminary data: MeCP2 binding tracks non-CG methylation and follows H2Aub. The distribution of MeCP2-GST binding in wild-type cerebellum closely mirrors methyl-CH (not methyl-CG) in published single-cell WGBS of granule neurons from adult mouse cerebellum56 (Fig. 6A), establishing MeCP2 CUT&RUN as a readout of mCH. In mutants, MeCP2 binding is markedly increased (Fig. 6B). At euchromatic genes such as Slc13a1, H2Aub gain and H3K27ac loss are accompanied by increased MeCP2 binding (Fig. 7C); at heterochromatic regions of Auts2, in contrast, H2Aub, H3K27me3, and MeCP2 fall together (Fig. 7D). Aggregate analysis confirms that MeCP2 rises where H2Aub increases and falls where H2Aub declines (Fig. 7E).
Aim 1.1:  Define the developmental relationship between BAP1-dependent H2Aub redistribution and alterations in 5mC and 5hmC. We will profile H2Aub (CUT&RUN) and 5mC/5hmC (Biomodal evoC) in the cerebellum of Bap1f/f,Math1-cre mice at P6, P12, P22, and adult (P60). This comprehensive time course spans the window in which granule neurons undergo the major developmental events: cell cycle exit, post-mitotic differentiation and functional integration into mature neuronal circuits68. 
At each timepoint, we will test for differential methylation using the modality XPLR analysis pipeline13, which fits a binomial logistic regression per region and tests the genotype coefficient by Wald test, with sex as a covariate. The model tests 5mC and 5hmC independently across six GENCODE vM25 annotation classes (gene bodies, promoters, CpG islands, CpG shores, CpG shelves, and TSS regions), with significance defined by Benjamini-Hochberg FDR <0.05. We will examine these transitions from a gene-centric perspective, comparing the abundance of H2Aub with both 5mC and 5hmC within 20,969 protein-coding genes. To account for H2Aub’s important role regulating intergenic enhancers, we will also examine changes 5mC and 5hmC from the perspective of loci which harbor H2Aub, specifically within sites that undergo significant increases or decreases in H2Aub.  We will classify genes with significant changes in both 5mC and 5hmC by the direction of change in each context: we predict that loci that gain H2Aub will show coordinated 5mC increase and 5hmC decrease, while loci that lose H2Aub will show the reciprocal pattern. To ask whether changes in methylation follow H2Aub redistribution rather than co-occur with or precede it, we will run the differential analyses at each timepoint independently and record the earliest timepoint at which each mark (H2Aub, 5mC/5hmC) first reaches significance at each gene. We will test by permutation analysis whether the predicted ordering—H2Aub change before methylation change—occurs more frequently than expected by chance.
Aim 1.2: Profile non-CG methylation (mCH) across the same time course. Non-CG methylation (methyl-CH) is a neuronally enriched, DNMT3A-dependent mark16,17,69 whose relation to H2Aub has never been examined. We will apply two methods for detecting this modification along the same P6 to adult time course. 
CUT&RUN using MeCP2-GST in lieu of an antibody in the CUT&RUN assay faithfully detects regions harboring non-CG methylation, allowing us to use CUT&RUN followed by straightforward analyses of differential binding to identify regions where mCH is perturbed by BAP1 loss. However, for base-level resolution of mCH, we will also employ whole-genome bisulfite sequencing. We will examine mCH within both genes and enhancers, and in relation to sites that have significant changes in H2Aub. We will quantify mCH at the gene-body level by aggregating all CH (CHH and CHG) sites within each GENCODE vM25 protein-coding gene body per sample. We will test differential gene-body mCH using the edgeR quasi-likelihood F-test66 (Benjamini-Hochberg FDR <0.05), with genotype and sex as biological covariates and log(total_coverage) as a per-gene offset. We will incorporate the per-sample conversion rate at lambda phage methyl-CH sites as a technical covariate to adjust for between-sample differences in bisulfite conversion efficiency. Because methyl-CH occurs at low baseline levels, incomplete cytosine conversion is the dominant source of false-positive calls, and we therefore specify conversion quality control. Unmethylated lambda phage DNA will be spiked into every library and each library will be required to demonstrate at least 99% cytosine conversion in the CH context; libraries falling below this threshold will be re-prepared rather than analyzed. Individual methyl-CH calls will be made only at cytosines covered at 10x or greater and will require a binomial test against the per-sample conversion error rate rather than being called from raw read counts, so that the residual non-conversion rate is treated as the baseline against which methylation is judged. Because these controls set a floor on detectable methyl-CH, we will report the per-sample detection limit alongside the methyl-CH estimates.
Aim 1.3: Profile H3K27ac across the same time course. According to our central hypothesis, binding of endogenous MeCP2 recruits the NCoR complex to deacetylate H3K27ac18,42, an interaction that could explain the unexpected and presumably indirect effects of Bap1 loss on H3K27ac. We will perform CUT&RUN detection of H3K27ac during the same time course above. 
Aim 1.4: Test the requirement for DNMT3A in BAP1-dependent dysregulation of methyl-CH and H3K27ac. We will cross mice harboring floxed Dnmt3a allele onto the Bap1f/f,Math1-cre background to produce experimental Bap1f/f,Dnmt3af/+,Math1-cre compound mutant mice. The comparators will be Bap1f/f,Dnmt3a+/+,Math1-cre (positive control for dysregulation of methyl-CH and H3K27ac) alongside Bap1f/+,Dnmt3a+/+,Math1-cre mice (unaffected controls). We will then ask whether heterozygous loss of DNMT3A blocks the methylation changes that follow H2Aub redistribution at the sites mapped earlier in this Aim. Importantly, heterozygosity for a global mutant in Dnmt3a causes modest loss of neuronal methyl-CH of ~50% lower20,70 (within the exact range required to correct the gain of MeCP2-GST binding that occurs at most sites), but has no effect on methyl-CG. After generating mice of these genotypes, we will profile H2Aub, H3K27ac and MeCP2-GST binding using CUT&RUN. We will also perform evoC in this double mutant to test whether removing DNMT3A blunts BAP1-depenedent changes in 5mC/ 5hmC. 
Aim 1.5: Determine whether Dnmt3a heterozygosity rescues transcriptomic changes of BAP1 loss. According to our model, loci that gain H2Aub also gain repressive methyl-CG and methyl-CH while losing the activating modifications 5hmC and H3K27ac, contributing to inappropriate repression of these loci. Heterochromatic loci lose H2Aub and experience epigenetic dysregulation in the opposite direction. To test the ability of partial loss of DNMT3A to rescue gene expression in Bap1 mutant neurons, we will isolate nuclei and perform RNA-seq to assess the nuclear transcriptome. 
Aim 1 expected outcomes, caveats & pitfalls, and additional experiments. We anticipate that H2Aub redistribution is the initiating event from which all other epigenetic consequences originate. Therefore, perturbation of H2Aub should precede changes in 5mC and 5hmC, consistent with an instructed rather than an independently regulated DNA methylome. However, temporal precedence alone is correlative, which is why we also include the epistasis experiment in which we mutate Dnmt3a to provide the causal mechanism that is central to this aim. We expect DNMT3A loss to blunt methylation changes and H3K27ac depletion at loci which gain H2Aub, resulting in correction of the inappropriate repression of these loci. If methylation changes fail to follow H2Aub redistribution in the predicted order, this would argue against a strict instructive hierarchy and instead support a model of parallel, co-regulated chromatin and DNA methylation change—a distinction our densely sampled time course is well powered to make, and one that would itself refine the model tested in Aims 2 and 3. A potential caveat is DNMT3A’s functional redundancy with DNMT3B. However, DNMT3B is expressed at much lower levels in cerebellar granule neurons71, both in our own datasets6 and those from other labs72.
Aim 2: Determine whether BAP1 catalytic-domain mutations remodel DNA methylation through H2Aub in human neurons.
    Aim 2 rationale. Aim 1 will establish the H2Aub-DNMT3A-methylation hierarchy using a mutant mouse with conditional loss of Bap1 in a specific population of neurons. Patients, however, carry heterozygous missense mutations in the ubiquitin C-terminal hydrolase (UCH) catalytic domain, not complete loss elimination of the BAP1 protein1,73. Furthermore, the neurodevelopmental disease mechanism is best tested in a human, isogenic background that removes confounds of genetic variation between patients and controls. This aim tests whether the mechanism defined in Aim 1 generalizes to human mutations that are directly relevant to BAP1-related NDDs.
Aim 2 preliminary data. Our preliminary base editing screens have identified an optimal base editor-gRNA combination for efficient introduction of patient-specific BAP1 mutations in HEK293T cells. We have made two mutations selected for this aim: exon 4 c.146T>C (p.Leu49Pro), carried by individual 4, and the recurrent mutation in exon 7 c.506A>G (p.His169Arg) carried by individuals 9 and 10 in the published cohort1. Individual 4 has a comparatively mild presentation, while individual 9 is more severely affected, with profound intellectual disability, very short stature and congenital malformations. His169 sits in the UCH catalytic pocket alongside the Cys91 in the active site74, and the His169Arg mutation abolished H2Aub deubiquitination in a heterologous rescue assay1. Amplicon sequencing of HEK293 cells showed efficient and precise editing of both sites—17.4% for Leu49Pro and 30.6% His169Arg of reads carried only the intended substitution (Fig. 8A-8D). 
Aim 2.1: Generate isogenic, patient-mutation NGN2 iPSC-neuron lines. We are introducing these two patient UCH-domain missense mutations separately into a doxycycline-inducible NGN2 human iPSC line by base editing75, generating isogenic mutant and wild-type control lines that differ only at the BAP1 locus. Plasmids encoding ABE8e-GFP76,77 and the corresponding gRNA will be introduced via lipoSTEM. GFP-positive transfected cells will then be isolated by flow sorting and plated at low density for manual selection of individual single-cell derived colonies. Successfully edited clones will be screened at the top five predicted off-target sites78 and assessed for normal karyotype and maintenance of pluripotency. We will confirm catalytic loss of function by mapping H2Aub using CUT&RUN prior to downstream profiling. To control for clonal variability and potential off-target editing effects, we will generate a minimum of three independent base-edited clones for each patient mutation, alongside isogenic wild-type clones derived from the same workflow. 
Aim 2.2: Profile chromatin and transcriptional consequences across neuronal differentiation. We will differentiate isogenic lines into excitatory neurons and profile H2Aub, H3K27ac, and H3K27me3 by CUT&RUN alongside RNA-seq at serial time points across differentiation, testing whether patient mutations reproduce the euchromatin/heterochromatin blurring—H2Aub and H3K27me3 gain with H3K27ac loss at euchromatic genes, and the reciprocal pattern at heterochromatic loci—that we observe in neurons of Bap1f/f,Math1-cre mice.
Aim 2.3: Profile DNA methylation and MeCP2 redistribution. We will apply evoC, WGBS, and MeCP2-GST CUT&RUN during the same differentiation time course, extending culture duration as needed to capture the slow accrual of non-CG methylation during neuronal maturation. Concordant, direction-matched changes in methylation and MeCP2-GST binding occupancy at loci that gain or lose H2Aub will establish that BAP1 controls the neuronal methylome through H2Aub in a human, disease-relevant genetic background. Because non-CG methylation is slow to accrue and could thus limit this aim, we will pre-specify methyl-CH levels as a milestone. In WGBS libraries we will quantify global methyl-CH/CH and the proportion of methyl-CH occurring in the CAC context, which emerges progressively during neuronal maturation79. We will require global methyl-CH/CH to be at least 0.1%—the level above which methyl-CH becomes anticorrelated with gene expression in neurons derived in vitro79—before interpreting genotype differences in methyl-CH or in MeCP2-GST binding. If this threshold is not met by day 42 of differentiation, we will invoke alternative approaches below.
Aim 2 alternative approach 1: accelerating neuronal maturation to allow for non-CG methylation. Luo et al. found that reprogramming mouse fibroblasts with Ascl1 produced 0.28% methyl-CH/CH by day 22, whereas adding Brn2 and Myt1l raised this to 0.57%, which is comparable to frontal cortex of two-week-old mice79. To our knowledge this was the first in vitro neuronal system to exceed methyl-CH/CH of 0.1%. Notably, Dnmt3a is strongly upregulated during this conversion and is functionally required for efficient reprogramming79, indicating that the factors driving maturation also induce the de novo methylation machinery central to our model. We would therefore generate a parallel set of induction lines, in the same isogenic BAP1-edited backgrounds, carrying doxycycline-inducible BRN2 (POU3F2) and MYT1L in addition to NGN2, following established human induced-neuron protocols80. MYT1L acts as a multi-lineage repressor that reinforces neuronal identity81, and its inclusion is the associated with methyl-CH accrual in mouse neurons. We would pair this with conventional maturation accelerants (co-culture on mouse glia, BrainPhys medium82, and extension of culture to day 56), and confirm DNMT3A induction by western blot at each time point. Two caveats are warranted. First, neurons derived in vitro acquire promoter hyper-CG methylation that is not observed in vivo79. Because our design compares mutant to isogenic wild-type processed in parallel, this signature affects both genotypes and should cancel in the differential analysis. As a result, we would not interpret promoter methylation levels in these cultures. Second, the scaling of methyl-CH with gene length is attenuated in reprogrammed neurons relative to cortex79, so long-gene analyses relevant to the MeCP2 axis will be anchored to the in vivo data from Aim 1.
Aim 2 alternative approach 2: modeling the patient mutations in mouse neurons. If methyl-CH accrual remains inadequate in human cultures, we would model the orthologous mutations in mouse neurons, which mature considerably faster in vitro. Leu49 and His169 lie within the UCH domain and are conserved between human and mouse BAP1, so the equivalent substitutions can be introduced into mouse embryonic stem cells using the ABE8e and gRNA workflow optimized in Aim 2.1. We will pursue two routes to neurons. In the first, edited ESCs would be differentiated through neural progenitors, which accumulate methyl-CH progressively in culture and reach approximately 0.34% by three weeks of differentiation79. In the second, we would derive fibroblasts from knock-in embryos and convert them directly to induced neurons using Ascl1, Brn2 and Myt1l79,83, the route that reaches cortical neuronal levels of methyl-CH most rapidly. The trade-off is the loss of the human genetic background. Because the anticipated direction of change is defined by Aim 1, a mouse system that reproduces the cascade using patient-orthologous point mutations would still substantially advance the central claim, whereas a human system that cannot make adequate methyl-CH cannot test it at all.
Aim 2 expected outcomes, caveats & pitfalls, and additional experiments. In our model, the phenotypes of Bap1 loss in mice result from perturbation of H2Aub, not a structural consequence of Bap1 loss (e.g. coordinating the formation of a chromatin-regulatory complex). Therefore, we expect patient UCH-domain mutations to reproduce the cascade of epigenetic phenotypes—H2Aub redistribution, reciprocal methylation change, MeCP2 redistribution, and H3K27ac loss—that occur in mouse neurons. We predict that the two mutations will produce concordant changes that may differ in magnitude, consistent with an allele series from patients with differing severity. If we fail to identify a similar suite of changes as we observe in Bap1 mutant mouse neurons, then we will generate neurons which are homozygous for each mutation. The efficiency of our gRNAs is high enough that this should be feasible without ay specialized techniques. 
An important consideration when working with iPSC-neuron models is heterogeneity and relative immaturity compared to neurons in vivo. We address this with a synchronized, inducible NGN2 system, independent isogenic clones per genotype, and extended duration in culture. If human neurons fail to show a similar cascade as mouse neurons, this could indicate a species-specific difference in DNMT3A regulation or in the H2Aub-DNMT3A interaction, which could represent reduced cross-species conservation of this pathway.
Aim 3: Ascertain whether restoring non-CG methylation rescues seizures caused by BAP1 loss.
Aim 3 rationale. Aims 1 and 2 will establish how BAP1 loss redirects non-CG methylation because of H2Aub redistribution in neurons. But these aims cannot determine that this change in DNA methylation is functionally required for any neurological phenotype. This aim performs an in vivo epistasis test: partially reducing non-CG methylation on a Bap1 mutant background isolates whether this specific molecular change is causally sufficient to modify a defined, patient-relevant phenotype. We focus on seizures because, unlike the developmental delay and broader behavioral abnormalities also present in Bap1f/f,Emx1-cre mice, seizures can be quantitated and are unconfounded by the relatively subtle behavioral deficits of Dnmt3a+/- mice. 
Aim 3 preliminary data. We found that deleting BAP1 specifically in excitatory neurons of the forebrain via Emx1-cre produces developmental delay by age P6 and overall growth delay during the juvenile period (Fig. 9A, 9B). Behavioral analysis demonstrated a stark and highly penetrant reduction in exploratory behavior in the open field test (Fig. 9C). Careful observation demonstrated that mutant mice appeared to experience absence seizure-like episodes. Therefore, we carried out continuous electroencephalography (EEG) recordings in awake behaving animals, measurements which demonstrated that mutants experienced thousands of “spikes” epileptiform discharges, which appear when large population of cortical neurons fire synchronously (Fig. 9D). We collaborated with Kim Dore’s lab at UCSD to perform patch clamp recordings in acute hippocampal slices. These experiments demonstrated reduction in the rheobase (the minimum current injection necessary to trigger an action potential) (Fig. 9E, 9F). We also observed increased numbers of action potentials evoked by the same current injection protocols (Fig. 9G). Importantly, Bap1f/f,Emx1-cre mice do not exhibit neurodegeneration at the histologic or phenotypic levels, and these mice typically live for more than one year. These observations indicate that BAP1 expression is not necessary for neuronal survival, but rather loss of BAP1 alters the fundamental activity of neurons by rendering them hyperexcitable. 
Aim 3.1: Determine whether Dnmt3a heterozygosity rescues developmental delay and behavior of Bap1 mutants. We will cross Bap1f/f,Emx1-cre mice to mice harboring floxed Dnmt3a to generate experimental Bap1f/f,Dnmt3af/+,Emx1-cre compound mutant mice. The comparators will be Bap1f/f,Dnmt3a+/+,Emx1-cre (positive control for developmental delay) alongside Bap1f/+,Dnmt3a+/+,Emx1-cre mice (unaffected controls). 
It is remarkable that loss of Bap1 in the forebrain is sufficient to induce global developmental delay. According to our central hypothesis, this results from impaired neurodevelopment via disruption of an epigenetic cascade that operates through non-CG DNA methylation. We will measure the growth of the lines of the genotypes of mice listed above. We will also test rescue of the exploratory behavior in the open field. Although Dnmt3a global heterozygous mice (Dnmt3a+/-) do exhibit subtle abnormalities in the open field test20, this should not interfere with our ability to observe rescue of the dramatic loss of exploratory behavior in Bap1 mutant mice. 
Aim 3.2: Determine whether Dnmt3a heterozygosity rescues neuronal hyperexcitability and seizures. While Dnmt3a+/- mice do exhibit subtle behavioral deficits, seizures are not among them. This makes it highly unlikely that heterozygous loss of Dnmt3a in excitatory forebrain neurons will per se cause seizures. This makes them a suitable model for us to assess the mechanistic basis of non-CG methylation in the neuronal hyperexcitability phenotype of Bap1 mutant mice, as this readout is specific to the epistatic interaction between Bap1 and Dnmt3a rather than to the general health of Dnmt3a+/- animals. We will perform continuous video-EEG monitoring across all the genotypes listed above genotypes to quantify seizure frequency, duration, and severity, testing whether Dnmt3a heterozygosity modifies the seizure phenotype of Bap1f/f,Emx1-cre mice. We will then generate acute hippocampal slices to perform electrophysiologic recordings from pyramidal neurons, with particular eye toward rescue of phenotypes associated with hyperexcitability, including the rheobase and the number of action potentials generated in response to current injection protocols. 
Aim 3: expected outcomes, caveats & pitfalls, and additional experiments. We predict that correcting the levels of non-CG methylation in Bap1f/f,Dnmt3af/+,Emx1-cre mice will rescue growth delay of Bap1f/f,Emx1-cre mice, while also attenuating their seizure phenotype and electrophysiologic signs of hyperexcitability. This would establish non-CG methylation as a causally required, in BAP1-related neurodevelopmental disease and identify DNMT3A, or its downstream effectors, as a candidate therapeutic target. A key caveat is that a partial reduction in DNMT3A dosage may be insufficient to produce a detectable rescue even if the underlying mechanism is correct. A second caveat is that epistatic interactions are not always linear; we will therefore treat growth delay and seizure severity as quantitative, correlated variables across the cohort rather than relying solely on a categorical comparison between genotypes.
