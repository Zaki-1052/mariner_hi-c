The goal of this project is to define how the histone deubiquitinase Bap1 regulates three-dimensional (3D) genome architecture during neurodevelopment. Preliminary CUT&RUN profiling in the cerebellum demonstrated that loss of Bap1 leads to elevated H2AK119ub deposition accompanied by widespread depletion of H3K27ac, particularly at genomic regions exhibiting increased H2AK119ub enrichment. These findings suggested that Bap1-dependent regulation of histone ubiquitination may influence higher-order chromatin organization and enhancer activity during neuronal differentiation.

To investigate this, we successfully established and optimized Hi-C from isolated cerebellar granule nuclei and generated deeply sequenced Hi-C libraries (approximately 900 million–1 billion paired-end reads per sample; ~30–40 GB per FASTQ). ACCESS computational resources on SDSC Expanse were utilized to implement a comprehensive Hi-C processing and analysis workflow. Primary processing was performed using HiC-Pro and Juicer for alignment, normalization, and contact matrix generation, followed by secondary analyses examining differential topologically associating domains (TADs), chromatin compartments, loops, and interaction frequencies across the genome.

These analyses revealed extensive genome-wide chromatin reorganization following Bap1 loss. Approximately 21% of TADs displayed differential interaction density, with the majority exhibiting increased interaction strength within heterochromatic regions. In parallel, global compartmentalization was weakened, with nearly 40% of 25-kb bins showing altered compartment strength, indicative of a blurred distinction between euchromatic (A) and heterochromatic (B) compartments. Chromatin loop analysis further identified approximately 20% differential loops, the majority of which were weakened or lost. Notably, these disrupted loops were enriched for enhancer–enhancer interactions, suggesting widespread super-enhancer dysregulation in the absence of Bap1.

Integration of Hi-C and CUT&RUN datasets further resolved differential loops into two major categories: cis-regulatory element (CRE) loops and polycomb/structural loops. CRE loops were defined by H3K27ac enrichment and the presence of CTCF at at least one anchor, whereas polycomb/structural loops were characterized by H3K27me3 enrichment with or without CTCF occupancy. Polycomb-associated loops exhibited a striking distance-dependent behavior, with strengthened short-range interactions and loss of long-range interactions. Increased short-range polycomb looping correlated strongly with strengthened local TAD architecture, likely contributing to the predominance of increased-density TADs observed genome-wide. In contrast, CRE-associated loops were broadly weakened or lost following Bap1 depletion. Together, these findings support a model in which aberrant accumulation of H2AK119ub disrupts the balance between long-range CRE interactions and short-range polycomb-mediated chromatin organization. Specifically, elevated H2AK119ub appears to favor local polycomb compaction while destabilizing enhancer-associated chromatin interactions, thereby reshaping 3D genome architecture during neurodevelopment. These results are currently being prepared for manuscript submission within the next several months.

Future work will extend these findings in two major directions. First, additional ACCESS-supported computational analyses will be used to characterize DNA methylation states in Bap1 mutant mice using evoC datasets (estimated 300 million reads per sample; ~10 GB per FASTQ). Second, we will define earlier developmental chromatin remodeling events using an in vitro differentiation system spanning mESCs, neural progenitor cells (NPCs), and neurons. These studies will incorporate both activation and depletion models of H2AK119ub to determine how altered histone ubiquitination dynamics influence 3D genome organization throughout neuronal lineage progression.
